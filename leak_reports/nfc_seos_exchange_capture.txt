==========================================================

ISO14443-4A card, replying 9000 to keep the reader talking. This is the reader's
own credential-hunt sequence over NFC. Reader -> emulated card, full hex:

The proprietary command (previously only partially captured):
  CLA=90 INS=5A P1P2=0000 Lc=03 DATA=000000 | B0BBBB   Le=00
  Sent immediately after the SEOS ADF list, twice with different 3-byte data.
  Purpose not definitively identified (HID-proprietary; obfuscated in the app);
  characterized here by structure. Our 9000 reply does not satisfy it, so the
  reader abandons the SEOS applet and moves to the next credential type.

WHY NO AKE CHALLENGE WAS CAPTURED
---------------------------------
The reader never issues its SEOS authenticated key exchange (AKE) challenge,
because it never gets a valid response to the `90 5A` gate (or a valid SEOS ADF
selection response) from our emulator. It selects STANDARD_SEOS, lists ADFs,
sends `90 5A`, gets a junk 9000, and gives up on SEOS -- then tries the HID
mobile credential AID, the mobile admin card, and the operation selector in
turn, failing each the same way. To reach the AKE we would have to answer
`90 5A` and the SEOS ADF selection with valid card-side responses, which
requires the SEOS card-side protocol details (obfuscated) AND, ultimately, the
credential's keys to answer the AKE cryptogram -- neither of which we have. The
attached SAM is a reader-side (terminal) CCID SAM used by seader to *read*
credentials, not a card-side crypto oracle, so it cannot produce these
responses. See MOBILE_KEYS.md and PROTOCOL.md sec 22.
